An accounting policy that has quietly been wrong for two years does not announce itself until an outside audit firm asks the one question that unravels it.

**A simplification that stopped being safe**

Carewave sells multi-year implementation and subscription contracts to hospital systems and outpatient clinics, and for two years its finance team had recognized that revenue on a simplified straight-line basis, treating the services and subscription components as one blended stream. It was a reasonable shortcut at an earlier stage. It was not a shortcut that would survive a real audit.

**The conversation that changed the timeline**

An early scoping conversation with Carewave's incoming audit firm made the gap explicit: recognizing services and subscription revenue separately, as the accounting standard actually requires, would produce materially different revenue figures for prior periods. Left uncorrected, that gap would have surfaced during fieldwork as a restatement, the kind of finding that adds months to an audit and puts real pressure on an IPO-track timeline.

**Rebuilding recognition before the audit firm arrived**

We rebuilt the revenue recognition policy from the ground up, going contract by contract to separate the implementation services and subscription components, then recalculated two years of historical revenue under the corrected methodology before the audit firm's first day of fieldwork. Alongside that, we redesigned the monthly close checklist and account reconciliation process so it would hold up under a genuine audit trail, not just an internal review that had missed the issue the first time.

**Results**

The audit-readiness review closed with zero material findings and no restatement required, keeping Carewave's IPO-track timeline intact and giving the board a close process built to survive the scrutiny still ahead of it.
